In the summer of 2022 me and my grandpa, who has worked as a carpenter most of his life, built a kitchen table for my apartment together. He suggested that it could be fun to create something together. Of course I said yes and travelled up to my grandparents house.

We got started early in the morning, planning the build. Over the span of two days we cut, sanded and glued together planks of wood to create the tabletop aswell as creating the legs. It was a really fun process. It requires a great attention to detail and focus which makes it a bit therapeutic. You are just in your head and nothing matters more in the world than the bit of wood in front of you.

<img>

After the project was done I didn't really have any plans to continue with woodworking, it was just a fun weekend project for me and a way to spend time with my grandparents. But as it turns out, the future had other plans for me.

---

#### Doomscrolling.

A couple of months ago during a long session of scrolling on my phone, I caught myself thinking: What am I even looking at right now? It struck me that I don't remember anything about what I just watched about 3 posts up from where I was.

I was scrolling endlessly, barely reacting or reflecting about the content I was watching. I didn't have a specific goal, nor was I watching a specific creator I like. I was scrolling on autopilot. I stopped, and felt terrible. I had done this before many times, but this time it felt concerning.

The culprit was Youtube Shorts.

I started thinking about what I could be doing with this time instead. A better way to relax and rest. A better way to get some entertainment. What do I want to do with my free time? I could take a nap, go for a walk, watch a tv show that's been in my watchlist for years or play one of the hundreds of games in my steam library. I have a bunch of hobby projects already on the shelf. Fun code projects I started but never finished. Working on my homelab. Some things require a lot of energy and focus, some require little to no energy at all and they were just waiting for me to put down the phone.

Sure, it's good to take a break do something that doesn't matter for a while, just to relax. But after scrolling, I did not feel relaxed. More than anything I felt slightly anxious and a bit uneasy that I had spent a couple of hours lying on my sofa watching my phone without any recollection of what I had watched.

---

#### The workshop

A couple of days goes by and I'm back scrolling on the recommendation page of Youtube again.

I am following a couple of woodworking channels on youtube but my feed hasn't given me any of those types of videos for while. But there it was, a video from [Jesper Makes](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1Vl7WQqodFc), a channel I had never seen before. It was great, I can definately recommend it. In hindsight this is a bit freaky, it's as if the algorithm knows what you want to watch sometimes.

The next week I was hanging out with a couple of friends. We were chatting and the topic of doomscrolling came up. I mentioned briefly about what I experienced when doomscrolling. And I mentioned jokingly the woodworking video I stumbled upon.

One of my friends mentioned he is also interested of woodworking. We started playing with the idea of getting a workshop together so that we can get started with woodworking together. I think we were both half joking and half serious when we were discussing it. And I guess he felt it aswell because a couple of days later he sent me a link to a workshop close where we live, affordable and great size for the cost.

One thing led to another, and we started renting this workshop together.

---

So here we are.

#### Woodworking. 

What do you even do? I need tools, and some workbench to work on. I started planning, taking notes, creating shopping lists. Tools are expensive, workbenches aren't cheap either - unless you build them yourself. **Spoilers:** That's exactly what I did.

In the video from Jesper Makes I linked above, he talked about using resources that you already have and ways of acquiring cheap wood to build stuff with. One of the things that I was really inspired by is how Jesper mentioned that companies usually throw away old wood pallets and things that products ship on. And that you can pull out of the spike of these with a crowbar and some elbow grease to get (sometimes) perfect wood planks to build things with.

This is where I got started. I asked my roommate if his company had some wood pallets they wanted to get rid of. He said yes.
