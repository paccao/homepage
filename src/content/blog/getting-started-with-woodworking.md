Last summer me and my grandpa, who has worked as a carpenter most of his life, built a kitchen table for my apartment together. He suggested that it could be fun to create something together. Of course I said yes and travelled up to my grandparents house.

We got started early in the morning, planning the build. Over the span of two days we cut, sanded and glued together planks of wood to create the tabletop aswell as creating the legs. It was a really fun process working together with my grandpa. It requires a great attention to detail and focus which makes it a bit therapeutic. You are just in your head and nothing matters more in the world than the bit of wood in front of you.

The end result was great

A couple of months ago during a long session of scrolling on my phone, I caught myself thinking: What am I even looking at right now? It struck me that I don't remember anything about what I just watched about 3 posts up from where I was.

I was scrolling endlessly, barely reacting or reflecting about the content I was watching. I didn't have a specific goal, nor was I watching a specific creator I like. I was scrolling on autopilot. I stopped, and I felt bad because this was a bit concerning to me.

The culprit was Youtube Shorts.

I started thinking about what I could be doing with this time instead. A better way to relax and rest. A better way to get some entertainment. What do I want to do with my free time? I could take a nap, go for a walk, watch a tv show that's been in my watchlist for years or play one of the hundreds of games in my steam library. I have a bunch of hobby projects already on the shelf. Fun code projects I started but never finished. Working on my homelab. Some things require a lot of energy and focus, some require little to no energy at all and they were just waiting for me to put down the phone.

Sure, it's good to take a break do something that doesn't matter for a while, just to relax. But after scrolling, I did not feel relaxed. More than anything I felt slightly anxious and a bit uneasy that I had spent a couple of hours lying on my sofa watching my phone without much of a thought about what I watched.

This was concerning. But it was also a wake-up call for me.
